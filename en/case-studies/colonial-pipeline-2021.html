Case study · Energy

Colonial Pipeline: ransomware that cut the fuel supply

United States, 2021. A compromised VPN password leads to the shutdown of the country's largest refined-fuel pipeline.

Sector
Fuel transport
Country
United States
Date
May 2021
Duration
Pipeline stopped 7–12 May
Ransom
About $4.4m ($2.3m recovered)

Analysis based solely on public information. FZ Consultation was not involved in this incident and has no affiliation with the organizations named.

What happened

  1. 29 April 2021

    Attackers gain access to the company's IT network, nine days before the attack.

  2. 7 May 2021

    An operator finds a ransom note. The company shuts down its entire 5,500-mile pipeline as a precaution, even though operational systems were not directly hit.

  3. 7 May 2021

    The company pays a ransom of about $4.4m in bitcoin for a decryption tool.

  4. 12 May 2021

    The pipeline restarts, after fuel shortages and price spikes on the East Coast.

  5. 7 June 2021

    The Justice Department announces it recovered about $2.3m of the ransom.

What went wrong

According to the public sources listed below.

Poorly protected remote access

Initial access came through a compromised VPN password, on an account that, according to public reports, had no multi-factor authentication and was no longer in use.

An uncertain IT/OT boundary

Without certainty about the extent of the compromise, the company stopped physical operations although only the IT network was hit.

Recovery dependent on the ransom

The ransom was paid to speed up recovery, a sign that restoring from backups alone could not meet the required timeframe.

Lessons for your organization

  1. 1

    MFA on every remote access

    And regular removal of inactive accounts, especially those with VPN access.

    Cybersecurity
  2. 2

    Segment and know your dependencies

    Know in advance whether operations can continue when IT is isolated, and on what criteria to stop.

    Business continuity
  3. 3

    Be able to restore without paying

    Isolated backups and tested restores give you the choice not to negotiate.

    Disaster recovery
