Case study · Healthcare

Change Healthcare: one portal without MFA stalls US medical billing

United States, 2024. Ransomware hits a central intermediary of the healthcare system and disrupts pharmacies and providers for months.

Sector
Healthcare transaction processing
Country
United States
Date
February 2024
People affected
About 192.7 million (2025 estimate)
Ransom
$22m paid, without preventing a second extortion

Analysis based solely on public information. FZ Consultation was not involved in this incident and has no affiliation with the organizations named.

What happened

  1. 12 February 2024

    Attackers log in to a Citrix remote access portal with compromised credentials. The portal did not require multi-factor authentication.

  2. 12–21 February 2024

    They move through the network and exfiltrate data for about nine days.

  3. 21 February 2024

    Ransomware is deployed. The company shuts down its data centres, halting electronic exchanges between providers, payers and pharmacies.

  4. March–April 2024

    A $22m ransom is paid. An affiliate nevertheless keeps the data, and a second extortion is launched by another group.

  5. May 2024

    The parent company's CEO testifies before Congress and confirms MFA was missing on the portal.

What went wrong

According to the public sources listed below.

An exception to the rule

According to the CEO's testimony, MFA was company policy for external systems, but this portal was an exception.

Too central a player

The company processes a major share of US healthcare transactions: its outage became an outage for a whole sector.

Paying doesn't protect you

The ransom did not stop the data from being kept, nor a new extortion attempt.

Lessons for your organization

  1. 1

    Verify, don't assume

    Regularly check that every exposed access point really enforces MFA, including after an acquisition.

    Cybersecurity
  2. 2

    Plan for losing an intermediary

    Identify suppliers whose outage would stop your business and prepare fallbacks.

    Business continuity
  3. 3

    Plan recovery without a ransom

    Isolated, tested backups are the only guarantee of recovery.

    Disaster recovery
