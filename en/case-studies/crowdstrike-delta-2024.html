Case study · Aviation

The CrowdStrike outage: when one update grounds the planes

Worldwide, 2024. A faulty security software update crashes 8.5 million Windows devices. Delta takes five days to recover.

Sector
Aviation and all sectors
Scope
Worldwide
Date
19 July 2024
Devices affected
About 8.5 million (Microsoft estimate)
Nature
Software error, not a cyberattack

Analysis based solely on public information. FZ Consultation was not involved in this incident and has no affiliation with the organizations named.

What happened

  1. 19 July 2024, 04:09 UTC

    CrowdStrike releases a configuration update containing a logic error for its Falcon sensor on Windows.

  2. 05:27 UTC

    The update is pulled, but machines already hit stay down and often have to be fixed one by one.

  3. 19 July

    Airlines, banks, hospitals and emergency services are disrupted; thousands of flights are cancelled.

  4. 19–24 July

    Delta says it cancelled about 7,000 flights affecting 1.3 million passengers, at a cost of about $500m.

  5. October 2024

    Delta sues CrowdStrike; CrowdStrike disputes responsibility.

What went wrong

According to the public sources listed below.

Deployed everywhere at once

The update reached all affected machines at once, with no staged rollout to catch the error.

Manual repair at scale

Many machines had to be booted into safe mode, sometimes with their BitLocker key, one by one.

Very uneven recoveries

Some airlines recovered in a day, others took several, depending on how well their operational systems could catch up.

Lessons for your organization

  1. 1

    Treat vendors as critical dependencies

    A security tool installed everywhere is also a single point of failure.

    Cloud and infrastructure resilience
  2. 2

    Plan recovery at scale

    Access to recovery keys, mass procedures and staff you can mobilize must be ready.

    Disaster recovery
  3. 3

    Test operational catch-up

    Beyond systems, rehearse your ability to reschedule crews, stock or appointments after the outage.

    Exercises and testing
