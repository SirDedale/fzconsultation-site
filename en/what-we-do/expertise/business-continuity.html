Expertise

Business continuity

Know what must keep going, in what order and with whom, before the crisis starts.

What we cover

A useful continuity plan is not only about IT. It covers people, sites, suppliers and communication. We start from your essential services and work back to the dependencies that can interrupt them.

Business impact analysis

We quantify how an interruption hurts over time, hour by hour rather than as a single blanket estimate. That makes it possible to set acceptable recovery times per service, rather than one target for the whole organization.

Dependency mapping

We link each essential service to the teams, systems, sites and suppliers it actually depends on, not just what the technical org chart suggests. This map then becomes the foundation for every continuity and recovery plan you build.

Continuity plans

We write short, actionable plans, meant to be followed during an incident rather than filed away. They align with ISO 22301 where that matters to your organization or your clients.

Crisis management and communication

We define the roles and decision chain of your crisis team, so everyone knows who decides what. Messages for staff, clients and regulators are prepared in advance, to save precious time on the day they are actually needed.

Critical suppliers

We assess the resilience of your key suppliers, beyond what their contracts promise. Where a supplier carries too much risk, we help you prepare realistic alternatives.

What you get

  • Business impact analysis report
  • Register of essential services and dependencies
  • Continuity plan and crisis management plan
  • Crisis communication templates

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between business continuity and disaster recovery?

Business continuity is about keeping the whole organization operating. Disaster recovery is one part of it, focused on restoring systems and data.

How often should a plan be updated?

At least once a year, and after any significant change: a new system, a new supplier, a reorganization or a real incident.
