FZ AI Auditor: audit of deployed AI agents

Scope, value realized and blast radius: three questions most organizations cannot answer with evidence.

Discuss your scopeDownload the scorecard

The problem

AI's advantage is that it scales a solution to every case at once. That is also its exposure: it scales whatever the solution actually is, mistakes included, and some mistakes are critical. Almost every organization can list the agents and automations it has deployed. Almost none can say, with evidence, what those agents actually do versus what they were authorized to do, whether the result is worth what it costs, and how much damage one bad decision could do before a human notices. FZ AI Auditor answers those three questions with evidence, on a cadence.

What the audit covers

Scope fidelity

What the agent was authorized to do, versus what it is actually doing in production, drawn from execution traces and logs rather than the design document. Scope drift is the normal state of a deployed agent; the real question is whether anyone is watching it.

Value realized

Volume handled, cost to run (compute, oversight, incident cleanup) against cost displaced, and a sampled quality review against a human-only baseline. A real number, not the business case that justified the build.

Blast radius

If this agent is wrong, how many cases does it touch, how severe is each one (reversible or irreversible, routine or financial, safety or compliance-critical), and how long before a human would catch it? This is the RTO/RPO question applied to an agent: how much can go wrong, and how fast can it be stopped.

Containment fit

The controls in place (approval gates, staged rollout, sampling review, a kill switch, a human owner) checked against what the blast-radius rating actually requires, not against what was built for convenience.

Why it matters

Mistakes multiply, not just output

A human error is one case. An agent error is every case it touched before someone looked: the same defect, repeated at the agent's volume.

Value claims age fast

The business case that justified an agent six months ago is rarely re-tested. Scope drift and model or prompt changes move the real number without anyone re-running it.

Regulators are starting to ask the audit question

The EU AI Act's post-market monitoring duties, ISO/IEC 42001, the NIST AI RMF and model-risk regimes applicable to financial institutions expect organizations to show what a deployed agent does and how it is overseen.

Frequently asked questions

Why audit an agent that was validated before launch?

Validation describes the agent on launch day. In production, scope, models, prompts and volumes change. The audit measures the agent as it is today.

What if we have few traces or logs?

The audit rests on evidence. Its absence is itself an important finding; we agree with you on the best way to close that gap before or during the audit.

Do you test on our production systems?

It depends on your risk tolerance. At scoping we choose with you a staging or shadow copy, or a simulation from logs.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the number of agents and their risk level. We discuss it in a 30-minute intro call and follow with a written proposal.

How is this different from AI governance?

Governance builds the framework: policies, inventory, roles and platform. The audit checks, with evidence, that deployed agents respect it and are worth what they cost. The two complement each other.

What does our team keep at the end?

The scorecard and method: a repeatable template so your team can re-run the audit each quarter.
