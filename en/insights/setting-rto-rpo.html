Setting RTO and RPO: start from the business, not the servers

How to choose realistic recovery objectives that business owners understand and IT can meet.

Recovery objectives are often set by IT, based on what the systems can do. It should be the other way round: the business says what it can bear, then you look at what it costs to get there.

Three questions for each service

  • After how long a stoppage does the damage become unacceptable: lost customers, missed obligations, safety of people?
  • How much recent data can we lose, and can we re-enter it by hand?
  • What happens during the outage: is there a degraded mode, a manual process?

Cost is the lever of the conversation

A fifteen-minute RTO does not cost the same as an eight-hour one. Putting a number on the cost of an hour of downtime, even a rough one, turns a technical discussion into an investment decision. Our order-of-magnitude calculator can be a starting point.

Common pitfalls

  • Labelling everything "critical": if everything is a priority, nothing is, and the budget will not follow.
  • Forgetting dependencies: a service does not restart before the identity, DNS or network it relies on.
  • Never measuring: an objective that has never been tested is an assumption.

Set simple tiers, get them approved by leadership, then test the highest-tier services first.
